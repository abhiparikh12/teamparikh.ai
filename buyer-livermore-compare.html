All three are older Livermore homes, and each package shows the ordinary wear you would expect for its age. None of them is clean, and none of them shows anything the reports call a reason to walk away. The differences come down to which big-ticket items are open, which have already been handled, and how complete each package is.

1162 Aberdeen Ave (1957, one story, pool) is the most "pre-handled." The seller completed the Section 1 termite work and HomeGuard issued a clearance on June 30, 2026, along with interior paint, carpet and some new windows. The main open items are a roof with an estimated 1–3 years of life and a Tesla (formerly SolarCity) solar Power Purchase Agreement the buyer would need to assume. ▶SolarSolar panels are under a Power Purchase Agreement the buyer would assume

The seller signed a SolarCity PPA on December 1, 2015; the seller now lists Tesla, which acquired SolarCity. Under a PPA, the solar company owns the panels and the homeowner buys the power they produce. The City permit for the 49-panel, 12.74 kW system was finaled January 20, 2016.

  • Rate: $0.1500/kWh in year one, rising 2.90% per year. Billed monthly on actual production (est. 15,813 kWh in year one).
  • Term: 20 years from the interconnection date. The seller's questionnaire gives installation and connection as 12/15 and the expiration as 2035. That puts the agreement in about its 11th year; by the contract's 2.90% escalator, the year-11 rate works out to roughly $0.20/kWh (our arithmetic, not a figure in the documents).
  • Seller's estimates: current solar payments of $120–250 per month, plus an annual PG&E true-up of $0–150. Net metering is in place; there is no battery. The seller indicates there is a transfer fee and a maintenance agreement, and refers to the agreement for details.
  • Transfer: buyer qualifies with a 650+ FICO, by paying cash, or by qualifying for a mortgage plus a $250 fee. 15 days to 3 months' written notice. Alternatives: seller prepays the remaining payments (at a 5% discount) or buys the system at appraised fair market value.
  • Roof work: the solar company removes and reinstalls panels for roof repairs for $499.
  • Recording: title shows a Notice of Independent Solar Energy Producer Contract recorded August 16, 2016. If the buyer assumes the PPA, this notice stays as an exception on the owner's and lender's policies. Title also shows a financing statement in favor of Tesla, Inc., recorded February 22, 2023 (debtor: Debbie Lavell). The documents do not say what it covers.
Consider requesting a current Tesla statement (rate, contract year, last 12 months of bills) and the transfer packet. Ask whether the seller intends to transfer the PPA or prepay it, and what the 2023 Tesla financing statement relates to. Include the $499 panel removal in any re-roof estimate, and check with the buyer's lender about its solar requirements.
