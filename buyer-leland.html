Executive Summary

This is a 1965-built, 3-bed/2-bath single-story home in Livermore's established Sunset West neighborhood, offered by the executor of the Virginia Kleiner Trust — a non-occupant who has acknowledged limited personal knowledge of the property's history. Three independent professional reports conducted in May 2026 document a meaningful list of conditions worth understanding as you shape your position in this transaction.

The property has genuine appeal: well-located in a walkable neighborhood close to downtown and the ACE train, cosmetically refreshed before listing, no HOA, no solar encumbrance, and clean title. Alongside those positives, the inspections surface several real items — a crawl space that couldn't be accessed due to moisture, an AC system that underperformed at inspection, garage-area water damage classified as Section 1 by the pest inspector, multiple electrical items needing attention, and a 16–20-year-old roof with 10 documented repair needs. This advisory helps you separate what's worth raising with the seller from what comes with the territory of a 61-year-old home and should factor into your offer price instead.

▶HVAC / CO RiskFurnace exhaust flue — moisture and corrosion observed
Moisture and corrosion at the exhaust flue is a legitimate safety concern — a compromised flue can route combustion gases into the living space rather than outside. A licensed HVAC technician needs to evaluate the flue and furnace. The furnace itself (Goodman forced air gas) is operational but older.
Requesting the seller provide an HVAC contractor's written inspection of the furnace and flue — with results shared — is a proportional and reasonable ask.
▶PlumbingMaster bathroom shower completely inoperable — no water output
The master bathroom shower has zero water output and cannot be operated. The pest report separately flagged the shower handle as inoperable. A licensed plumber needs to diagnose whether this is a failed valve cartridge or something more involved. A non-functional primary bath shower is a functional deficiency, not routine wear and tear.
A plumbing repair or equivalent credit is a reasonable, well-supported ask. Obtaining a plumber's estimate during your contingency period will anchor the conversation.
▶Plumbing / SafetyHall bathroom tub — hot and cold supply reversed
Hot and cold lines are plumbed backwards at the hall bathroom tub. Beyond inconvenience, this creates a scalding risk when someone reaches instinctively for the cold tap. This is a simple plumber correction at modest cost.
A reasonable item to ask the seller to correct prior to close.
▶ElectricalExposed wiring (garage + kitchen sink), non-functional garage GFCI
Three specific electrical corrections fall outside what can fairly be attributed to home age alone: (1) exposed wiring on garage walls below 7 feet needs conduit enclosure, (2) exposed wiring under the kitchen sink needs conduit enclosure, and (3) the GFCI outlet in the garage failed to trip — requiring circuit investigation and repair. The broader ungrounded-outlet issue throughout the home is a 1965 characteristic that belongs in the offer price, not this list.
The specific exposed wiring and failed GFCI are well-supported items to include in a repair request. The broader grounding upgrade is appropriately a long-term buyer project.

Repair Request Framework

A well-calibrated repair request focuses on material, functional, or safety items where seller participation is both reasonable and realistic — not a wholesale transfer of every inspection finding. The framework below separates what's worth raising with the seller from what belongs in the buyer's court. A focused, proportional request is far more effective than a comprehensive list that reads as an attempt to re-price the deal through the back door. The listing agent on the other side needs to be able to present this to their client as reasonable — that's how repairs actually get done.

The Right Posture

A focused Tier 1 request — the Section 1 pest items, all 10 roof repairs, the gas cap, the garage door, the reversed plumbing, the exposed wiring, and the GFCI — is clean, well-documented, and defensible. These have clear professional findings behind them and finite, reasonable costs. A listing agent can present this package to their seller without difficulty. Tier 2 items are best addressed through a combined closing credit that acknowledges the HVAC uncertainty and the crawl space unknown — asking the seller to manage contractors for complex diagnostic work is rarely practical in escrow and creates friction without resolution. Tier 3 items belong in the offer price relative to updated neighborhood comps. The long-term capital exposure — electrical upgrade, HVAC replacement, eventual roof — is precisely why this home should clear well below the modernized 469 Ontario Dr benchmark ($1,465,000 / $873/SF) and instead anchor closer to the primary 3/2 comp at 555 Ontario Dr ($1,350,000 / $811/SF), less a deferred-maintenance discount of $95,000–$200,000. That math defines the as-is offer band of ~$1,150,000–$1,250,000.
